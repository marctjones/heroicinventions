# The fastest route, end to end (issue #96)

`docs/lonely-rover.html` ("Routes to the call", "The fastest route") as a script that runs in the crater world, and what stops a player doing it by hand today.

- World: `game/worlds/lonely-rover-e2e.world` (the opening's cargo, rover and slide, the real found bank, `found-bank`, under about 1 m of rubble at (254.5, 137.9), and the `motors` crate, `found-motor`; scenario `bank-capacity 0.005`, the 5 kWh bank as 25 Wh).
- Vault machine: `racket/machines/route-vault.rkt`, placed round the found bank's crate: a tight regolith vault, the heated rock in a lidded bin, a bimetal strip on the lid (sensing the vault's air), a 4 m2 heliostat and the wakes; header has the reasoning. It is a machine of its own: the vault holds the found bank's cells because the crate's centre is inside its box (#211, `WorldZones`). The stand-in `found-bank-vaulted` (the vault written into the bank's own machine) is gone, as are the old placed works `e2e-route.rkt`: the windmill and train are built by the test, and the generator is the salvaged motor of the `motors` crate (`racket/machines/found-motor.rkt`), found, not built (owner ruling, 2026-10-09).
- Clock: the opening's and the found bank's own (no sun given: noon at the start, latitude 31.2, day 172; the sun sets near 17:00). The call is the found bank's 03:00, 15 local hours on: 55,484 s.
- Tests: `racket/heroic/tests/e2e-route-test.rkt` (builds, saves, wires, sleeps and wins, plus two gates; about 4 minutes, the three sleeps in parallel) and `e2e-rover-test.rkt` (the rover's drive and the dig attempts, about 12 s). The same route played through real input in a window: `tools/route-gui.sh` and the opt-in `route-gui-test.rkt` (see "The route played as a person", below); the scripted `join` step: `join-step-test.rkt` (about 15 s).

## The train: what ratio the generator needs

The sails' torque is tau = tau* (2 - w R / (v l*)), tau* = 104.0 N.m at 6 m/s in the world's air; the motor takes tau_g = 0.1146 (X - 157.08) N.m above its cut-in (157.08 rad/s, 1,500 rpm). With ratio G the rotor settles at X = (2 tau* + 157.08 G k) / (G k + 4 tau* / (v G)).

| Stages | Ratio | Rotor at 6 m/s | Lowest wind that passes the cut-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 16:1 | 80 rad/s, under | none |
| 3 | 64:1 | 161.5 rad/s (1,542 rpm), over by 4.4 rad/s | 4.9 m/s |
| 4 | 256:1 | 162.7 rad/s (1,553 rpm; sim 1,557), over by 5.6 | 1.2 m/s |

A three-stage train DOES pass the cut-in at the palette windmill's 6 m/s (checked in the sim: it ran the same route and won at 03:00, settling near 1,555 rpm and 80 W, still falling), against the brief's expectation that it would not. It has no margin below 4.9 m/s; the fourth stage is what keeps the generator charging in a weaker wind and is why the route builds four. The torque curve makes the result a small difference of two large numbers: the worked 83 W against the sim's 90 W is a 0.4 rad/s difference in the rotor's speed.

## GAPs

What a player cannot yet do, the issue it belongs to, and the stand-in used. "NEW" has no issue yet.

1. **Freeing the bank (changed; now optional).** The bank is under about 1 m of rubble since the owner's ruling of 2026-10-09, freeing it is optional (a vault can be built round it where it lies; the goals panel says so) and no boulder rests on it any more (the opening's boulder pins the solar-panels crate instead; this route's world is unchanged). The dirt rule landed: the backhoe digs the slide's rubble now (0.20 m3 a cycle at four places; `e2e-rover-test.rkt` prints them) where it was refused as bedrock. Not run: the cycles that clear the crate's rubble, and then pushing the crate into a vault. The crate is the found bank itself since #209 (its charge, cells and record go with it), so the old "crate is not a bank part" half of this gap is closed. Stand-in: the bank is not freed; the vault is at the crate.
2. **Done: a vault of another machine holds the found bank (#211).** An enclosure holds any machine's heat store whose point lies in its box (`WorldZones`, worked out each tick from where the stores are: the found bank's cells ride the crate their bank is `#:on`; the point decides for a crate straddling the wall; a store already in a room of its own machine stays there). Pushing the crate in or out joins or leaves the vault (`[zones]` in the log), a save keeps it (it follows from where the crate is), and `vault-zone-test.rkt` checks the doc's night: +4.28 C at 03:00, night-heat's +4.3. Residual: the route's vault is placed by the world round the crate where it lies, not dug by the rover (the rover's own vault digging is not done), and the crate is not freed and pushed into it (GAP 1). A vault the player builds in build mode is a placement like any other, so the same rule applies to it; that is not run here (the checks place theirs).
3. **Done: the windmill and train are built by the player** (since #204); the generator is found, not built (owner ruling, 2026-10-09: it is not in the game's parts list; the salvaged motor is in the `motors` crate), joined to the train's last pinion by a shaft link and wired to the found bank (#208), and the found bank charges and wins (#209). The palette gears give 4:1 a stage, so the train is four stages (see above). Residual: the palette windmill takes a flat 6 m/s and ignores the map's wind corridor and its night factor (the old placed works took both), so its charge rate is lower; it also faces a fixed +z (#193).
4. **Hot rock: heating it in the bin is a legitimate route (owner ruling, 2026-10-09), and this route takes it; pushing the rock in at dusk is the harder variant and the mechanism exists (#206).** A heat store can be a body (`#:movable #t`, a cube of its material: 40 kg of basalt is 0.24 m); a `heat-bin` with no `#:holds` stands empty and a movable store pushed in so that its middle is inside becomes its store; a mirror lights it only while it lies in its beam; the rover pushes it within its hands' rules (`racket/machines/heat-rock.rkt`, `heat-rock-test.rkt`: 200 C rock pushed in at 17:00, bank +4.2 C at 03:00 against night-heat's +4.3). The route heats the rock in the bin, which is the route most players will find; the dusk push leaves about 4 K of margin at 03:00 and ten minutes late costs it. A route that pushed the rock in would have a `#:movable` rock heated in the open to dusk and an empty bin, and a scripted sleep (#207) to hold it until dusk: not done here.
5. **Scripted sleep: done (#207).** `sleep WAKE [live|paused]` and `sleep until TARGET.FIELD above|below VALUE [limit S] [live|paused]` are `HEROIC_INPUT` steps (the first looks for the wake on the focused machine, then on every placed machine): they sleep the world as the panel does and hold the script until the wake, so a rover script can drive, build, sleep, then act in one run (`sleep-live-test.rkt` sleeps in this world). The route's own test still starts its sleep at load (`HEROIC_SLEEP`); it could use the step, which would let the build and the sleep share a run.
6. **The join gesture: done (#218, #225).** `join A B` is an input step (`LABEL.PART[.PORT]` each; `conventions.md`): it goes through the same pick path a click ends in, so the link is the one a click makes, anywhere in a script, with parts tens of metres apart; a wrong name or a refused join ends the run with exit 1 (`join-step-test.rkt`). Played with real clicks (`tools/route-gui.sh`, `joinclick`), the route's two links are made by the gesture itself: a click prefers the smaller of nested pick boxes (the last pinion inside the sails' box, the bank inside its crate's), the generator and the bank have boxes of their own, and a second click or a right-click lists the parts under it where boxes overlap (the generator over the rotor); see "Where the real route broke" below. The route's own test (`e2e-route-test.rkt`) still writes the links into the save, as before.
7. **Sleep and Jolt (#207): the engine can run through a sleep, at its own pace.** A sleep of a Jolt-driven machine (geared trains, driven shafts, axles, moving bodies) lets the physics engine run: the game's own loop at 200x with the redrawing left out, so every tick is a watched tick and the result is the same (generator-train 240 s, and the crater world's route machine over a 30 s sleep: every field of every row equal to the watched run's, max difference 0). The price is the engine's pace, about 600 steps a second in the crater world (a 600 s sleep took 120 s), so the route's 54,800 s sleep would take about 2.5 hours live. A sleep is live by default only while its estimate is within 7,200 s (`SleepControl.LiveLimit`); a longer one pauses the Jolt side as before, unless told to keep machines turning (the panel's "Keep machines turning", `live` in the step, `HEROIC_SLEEP_PHYSICS=1`). So the route as it stands still sleeps with the Jolt side paused; sleeping it with the windmill turning (`HEROIC_SLEEP_PHYSICS=1`) is possible but hours long, and the first hours (before the bank warms) charge nothing anyway. Left: a faster live night (a coarser Jolt step is not the same result as watching).
8. **Tooling: done (#207, except the trace field).** A trace sampled every `dt` skips to the next sample after a sleep instead of writing a frame per tick (generator-train, dt 1, a sleep to 200 s of 240 s with the engine paused: 42 rows, none inside the sleep). A sleep's autosave goes to `HEROIC_SAVES_DIR` when set (`godothost.rkt` gives every run a temporary one), and a scripted run (`HEROIC_QUIT_AFTER_SIM_SECONDS`) with none writes no autosave. A found bank's `from-wind` is not a trace field; the source record is read from the game's ending lines.

9. **A generator in another machine than its prime mover names its source `shaft`, and has no prime mover speed (new, from the salvaged motor).** `PrimeMoverOf` looks for the windmill or wheel inside the generator's own machine; across a world shaft link there is none, so the bank's record reads `shaft` where it read `wind`, and the concept achievement Gear up (a train of 100:1 or more) cannot read the prime mover's speed for the motor. A fix reads through the world's shaft links (`Electrics`, off-limits to this change). NEW.

Not done: the doc's Stirling engine, a second sol (the rover re-heating the rock each sol), the rover's own vault digging, the boulder undermining, the join gesture itself, the overheating gate in this world (electrics-test.rkt and bimetal-night own it), and the full 5 kWh bank (the scenario scales it to 25 Wh; it would take 55.6 hours of this windmill).

## A vault built by hand round the buried bank (#213)

Run as a person would, in the opening (the bank's crate at (254.5, 137.9) under 1.04 m of rubble), with `tools/gui-check.sh --hidden` (camera printed at each shot) and, for the numbers, `racket/heroic/tests/hand-vault-test.rkt`.

Steps, and where each broke:

1. Drive the rover to about (246.5, 137.9) facing +x, press **Build a new machine**. Build mode opens 8 m ahead *on the surface* at (254.5, 137.9): the machine's origin is the rubble's top (y -42.92), the crate's middle is 1.29 m below it. Works. A scripted `build new X Z` does the same anywhere.
2. **Broke: the palette shows only Beam, Block, Ball, Ramp, Post, Rope, Water tank.** The enclosure, heat store, heat bin, mirror and bimetal are under **Show all**. Nothing says so; left as it is (not a defect of the parts, a discoverability gap for the owner).
3. **Broke (fixed): the crate was not drawn and could not be picked.** It is another machine's body under opaque soil; the game's own tags ("crate buried 1.04 m", "bank 0.0/500 Wh -63 C too cold") were already there but a person cannot tell where to build from them. Build mode now draws, while it is open, a translucent box at the crate, a thin shaft up to the surface and the line "found bank lies here, 1.04 m down: build the vault round it" (`game/scripts/BuildMode.Buried.cs`; a child of build mode, so gone when it closes; not selectable, no physics). It also outlines any room of the machine being built that lies under the ground, since a part below the ground is hidden by it.
4. Place an enclosure with its floor 1.54 m below the origin, `#:at (0 -1.54 0)`, 0.5 m a side (the console or the inspector's numbers; a mouse click lands on the *surface*, so the depth is typed, or set after placing). **Works: build mode does place parts below the ground**, and the box holds the crate's middle: `[zones] battery-bank.cells joined built-1.vault` (#211, by position).
5. **Broke (fixed): the template's `wall` is `#f` and `insulation` 2.** A room with no wall is a bare membrane box. Worse, the template keeps the 2 W/K of *insulation* when a wall is given, where the DSL makes it 0 ("#:insulation is then 0 unless given"): the hand-built 0.5 m regolith vault leaked 2 W/K into the -63 C night and the bank was **-27.1 C** at 03:00 against the route's +4.28. Now, on a planet whose air is below freezing (`Planet.Temperature < 0`: Mars -63 C, not Earth 20 C; no new rule) an enclosure placed with no `#:wall` is placed with `#:wall regolith`, and a wall means `#:insulation 0` (written into the command the console shows; `BuildMode.WithPlaceDefaults`). Giving or taking away a wall on a room already there moves the insulation the same way (`WallChanged`). The player can change it: `#:wall granite`, `#:wall #f`, `#:insulation 2`, or the inspector's new **Wall** list (there was no way to set a wall from the inspector at all: it showed a number field for `#f`). `#:ground` is a *temperature* (the ground's, default the air's); `#t` means nothing, so "ground contact" is the wall itself and no default was added for it.
6. Add the rock (`heat-store`, 40 kg basalt, 200 C) and an open `heat-bin` round it, inside the box. Works. (The route heats the rock with a mirror; here the rock is given hot, as night-heat and vault-zone-test do.)
7. Leave build mode and sleep to 03:00. Works (`sleep until scene.elapsed above 37000 limit 40000 paused`).

Check against #211 (the vault holds the found bank by position). Predicted from `vault-zone-test.rkt` (night-heat's tight vault: 0.5 m cavity, 0.5 m regolith wall and ground at -55 C, 40 kg at 200 C, open bin 0.1 W/K, cells at -55 C): **+4.28 C** after 36,990 s. The hand-built vault, same numbers, traced: **+4.279 C** (the rock 28.7 C, the vault 5.5 C). With only the defaults (no `#:temperature`, `#:ground`: the air and ground at Mars's -63 C, cells starting at -63) the same vault gives **-3.4 C**: a colder ground is a colder night, a gameplay result, not a defect.

Not done: the mouse path (palette click, drag to a depth under the soil) was not run, only scripted `build` commands (the console path build mode's mouse uses); a mouse click places on the surface, so a person must type or edit the depth. The vault is not dug by the rover and the bank is not freed (GAP 1). The cleaner place for the cold-planet default is `BuildSession.CreatePrimitive`/`PartTemplates` (the planet is known there); it was kept in build mode's files.

### Every difference from the automated route, and what it means

1. **The links are made while the world runs, by clicks (#225), not written into the save.** In the automated route they are text written into the save before it loads. Here they are made after the build by real clicks (`joinclick`: the pinion, the rotor, the generator, the bank), so the shaft and wire exist from about t = 64 s (the rotor reads 13 to 138 rpm at noon with the wire on a cold bank: "not charging: bank -63 C"). Ids: `shaft-1`, `wire-1` where the test writes `drive-1`, `wire-1`. Same link forms (`join-step-test.rkt`). The `join A B` step by name stays for scripts that cannot click.
2. **The windmill is driven to, not placed.** `build new 180 105` there; the rover is driven from (190, 140) (turn left 3.2 s, up 10 s, turn right 0.8 s, up 4.6 s) and the button opens build mode 8 m ahead of it. Same place to about 0.2 m. A person would be off by metres: the wind there is a few percent different (the corridor factor).
3. **The post is a palette click, the rest is typed.** The post is placed with the palette's Post (a click in the entry, a click in the scene) at the clicked ground (about (180, 100)), then sized with `set` (a click places on the surface and sizes are typed: #223). The rule (`PickBoxes` in the sim, pinned by `PickBoxesTests`): boxes that do not hold one another, nearest first as before; when the nearest box holds others the ray also crossed, the smaller wins, and so on inwards (boxes of nearly equal size, one percent, count as equal and the nearer wins).
